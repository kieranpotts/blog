= Continuous adaptation
Kieran Potts
:description: We talk about continuous improvement, but the truer goal is continuous adaptation — constantly adjusting our processes and tools to circumstances that never stop changing.
:docinfo: shared
:nofooter:

Software development is really two activities woven together.

The first is the obvious one: implementing functional and non-functional requirements in software — building the thing.

The second is quieter but no less important: getting better at how we do the first. Improving the way we work.

We usually talk about that second activity as _continuous improvement_, and the phrase is fine as far as it goes. But I think it points us at the wrong target. "`Improvement`" implies a fixed standard we are climbing towards, as if there were some best practice waiting at the top of the hill that, once reached, we could settle into.

There isn't. The ground keeps moving. The needs of the business shift. Requirements change. Technologies advance and decay. Teams form, grow, and disperse. A practice that was exactly right for last year's project, last year's team, and last year's toolchain can be quietly wrong for this year's.

So the goal is not really improvement in the abstract. It is _adaptation_ — constantly adjusting our processes and our tools to fit the circumstances we actually find ourselves in. Less a climb towards a summit, more a continuous course correction. The teams that thrive over the long run are not the ones that found the "`best`" way of working and locked it in. They are the ones that never stopped adapting.
