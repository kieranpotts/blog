= Semantic diffusion
Kieran Potts
:description: Our most useful terms — agile, REST, OOP, unit test — tend to lose their meaning the moment they become popular. Martin Fowler called this semantic diffusion, and our industry is unusually prone to it.
:docinfo: shared
:nofooter:

Martin Fowler coined the term _semantic diffusion_ to describe the way a useful word gradually loses its precise meaning as it spreads through a community. Each retelling waters it down a little, until eventually the word means something much broader, and much vaguer, than its originators intended.

Our field is riddled with examples:

* *Continuous integration* has come to mean little more than "`running a build in the cloud`", rather than the discipline of integrating everyone's work frequently into a shared mainline.
* *DevOps* gets reduced to Docker and Kubernetes, rather than a culture of shared ownership across development and operations.
* *Unit test* is now used as a synonym for any automated test, including integration and system tests that make no attempt to isolate a unit under test or to deliver fast feedback.
* *TDD* is taken to mean "`writing automated tests`", rather than letting tests drive the design.
* *Refactoring* is taken to mean "`rewriting bits of code`", rather than the disciplined process of evolving a system's design over time without changing its behavior.
* *REST* and *RESTful* have been diluted to the point that they describe any HTTP-based API, including those that follow none of the principles of REST. (I've written about this elsewhere.)
* *MVC* is applied to any tiered application architecture.
* *OOP* is taken to mean "`using classes and objects`". But public get/set accessors over private fields are _not_ encapsulation; public methods should express behavior, not the data structure beneath it. There are exceptions — data transfer objects, value objects, simple domain models that really are mostly data — but OOP is fundamentally about object-oriented _thinking_ and _design_, not syntax.
* *FP* is taken to mean "`using map, filter, and reduce`".
* And perhaps worst of all, *agile* has been diluted until it means almost nothing — and it is certainly not the same thing as Scrum.
* *Software engineering* is treated as a synonym for software development. The term originated at the 1968 NATO Software Engineering Conference, a response to the software crisis of the day, and it encompasses a far broader set of practices and principles than "`writing software`".

Perhaps _AI_ will go the same way.

Happily, _domain-driven design_ seems to have escaped semantic diffusion so far — perhaps precisely because it has not been so widely adopted.

The practical consequence is that we keep having to invent new terms to describe the original concepts, because the old ones have been worn smooth.

Semantic diffusion happens in every domain — medicine, electronics, astronomy. But something about our field makes it especially acute. Unlike most professions, you don't need a formal qualification to land your first entry-level job in software. Around half the people in our industry — myself included — are self-taught. That means our first exposure to the industry's vocabulary tends to come not from textbooks or accredited courses, but from blog posts, conference talks, and YouTube videos: informal sources, where the diffusion has often already happened.
